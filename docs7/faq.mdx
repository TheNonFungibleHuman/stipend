---
title: FAQ
description: The questions people actually ask, answered without hedging.
---

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Is this a router that picks the cheapest vendor?">
    No. Nothing here chooses a vendor — your agent or your code decides. Stipend does the two things
    that decision cannot do for itself: it pays a vendor that has no account for you, and it refuses
    anything outside your limits before a signature exists.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="My model provider already bills per call. What does this add?">
    Different layer, and your model invoice is untouched. Your provider already bills per token and
    already caps spend. Stipend is for everything else the agent needs: search, rendering, OCR,
    datasets. Those normally want an account, a card on file and a monthly plan.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does the end user need their own AI key?">
    No. The vendors' reasoning is the vendor's cost, priced into the call. Your agent brings its own
    model. The Stipend key authorises spending, nothing else.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Who holds the agent's private key?">
    On the demo instance, the server does, so the flow works in a browser with no extension. In
    production the key lives where the agent runs and the same key gates a signing service or a
    policy program. The enforcement point moves; the guarantee does not.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What stops it from overspending?">
    Four checks between the quote and the signature: allowlist, per-call cap, rolling daily budget,
    balance. A refusal builds no transaction, pays no fee, and produces no signature.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is dUSD?">
    A devnet token with six decimals that the treasury mints, so an agent has something stable to
    spend while you try the product. It is not real money and it is not redeemable.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does a vendor need an account or a billing integration?">
    No. It publishes a price on the `402`, then reads the payee's balance on chain before answering.
    Payment and proof are the same object, so there is nothing to reconcile later.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can a vendor overcharge me?">
    It can quote anything. It cannot get paid above your per-call cap: the quote is checked before
    signing, so an inflated price becomes a refusal with a line in the ledger.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="When would I not need Stipend?">
    If your agent only ever calls one vendor that already bills per call and has its own spend limit,
    keep what you have. It earns its place when the vendor count rises or a vendor will not sell you
    a single call.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
