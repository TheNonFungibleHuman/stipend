---
title: Use cases
description: Six things an agent can buy per-call that it could not buy at all before.
---

Every case below has the same shape: a vendor that would normally require an account, a card on
file, and a plan you cannot cancel per call.

## 1 · Pricing and positioning research

An agent asked to write a launch memo buys live discussion about the category, distils it into
notes, and writes the memo. **~$0.18** for the whole run, versus a research seat per month.

## 2 · Competitive monitoring

A scheduled agent checks what changed in competitors' public pricing and release notes once a day.
At four cents a check, a month of daily monitoring costs about **$1.20** — cheaper than one seat,
and it does not need an account with any of the sites it reads.

## 3 · Due diligence on a token or a market

Before an agent acts on a market view, it buys current signal and a summary rather than trusting a
model's memory. The receipt is the provenance: you can show exactly what it read and when.

## 4 · Enriching a dataset, one record at a time

A pipeline needs an OCR pass, a page render, or a classification for each row. Per-record purchase
means you pay for the rows you actually process, and a failure stops at one record instead of
burning a monthly quota.

## 5 · Incident triage

An on-call agent buys a summary of what changed on a status page and a search for whether other
people are seeing the same failure. It pays for the two cents of data it needs at 3am, instead of a
human tabbing through dashboards.

## 6 · Giving a non-human buyer an actual budget

Any workflow where you want an agent to spend — but only inside limits you set, with a record of
every refusal — is the same shape. The vendor does not matter; the control does.
